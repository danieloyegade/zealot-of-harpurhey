COME THROUGH LAB, MANCHESTER

Untextured Blender reconstruction for Zealot of Harperhey

Create an accurate, game-ready Blender reconstruction of the Come
Through Lab premises in Manchester, using ALL supplied reference
photographs.

This asset is for Zealot of Harperhey, a Three.js game.

This task is GEOMETRY ONLY.

Do not create final textures, decals, logos, QR codes, printed
instructions, graffiti, brick textures, signage graphics or lighting
yet.

The goal is to reconstruct:

the historic brick facade;

entrance;

windows;

stone detailing;

security grilles;

number 84;

Come Through Lab drop box;

envelope/pencil holder beside it;

immediate architectural surroundings;

accurately enough that the building reads correctly in plain clay
materials.

⸻

1.  IMPORTANT GAMEPLAY REQUIREMENT

The Come Through Lab drop box is a functional gameplay object.

In the real location, customers can leave film for processing when the
lab is closed.

The later game mechanic should support approximately:

Player approaches the CTL drop box.

Player interacts with it.

Player can submit exposed film.

The game can reference an order/payment process.

Film is considered deposited.

Developed files become available later.

Negatives can subsequently be collected.

Do NOT implement any gameplay logic in Blender.

However, the drop box must be modelled as a distinct, well-organised
asset suitable for interaction.

⸻

2.  STUDY ALL REFERENCES BEFORE MODELLING

Cross-reference every supplied image.

The references provide:

a close frontal view of the entrance;

a close-up of the CTL drop box;

wider street views showing scale;

views showing more of the complete building;

views showing upper floors;

views showing the building’s relationship to the neighbouring industrial
architecture;

useful views of window spacing and facade rhythm.

Do not copy Google Street View lens distortion as geometry.

Infer a consistent architectural structure from all views.

⸻

3.  BUILDING SCOPE

Model enough of the building containing Come Through Lab to establish
the location convincingly.

Include:

ground floor;

upper floors visible in the references;

left edge/corner of the building;

entrance;

repeated ground-floor windows;

upper sash-style windows;

brick facade;

stone details;

projecting balcony sections where visible;

roof/parapet massing where visible.

Do not attempt to reconstruct the entire street.

Adjacent buildings, cars, fences, trees and modern structures should not
be fused into this asset.

⸻

4.  HISTORIC BRICK FACADE

The brick facade is an important part of the visual identity.

Do NOT simplify it into a generic perfectly flat red wall.

Preserve the main physical brick architecture using geometry where
appropriate.

Important geometry includes:

wall thickness;

recessed window openings;

projecting stone sills;

brick arches above windows;

alternating brick colour patterns within arches;

horizontal brick bands;

projecting brick piers;

changes in wall plane;

entrance surround;

balcony supports.

Do not model every individual brick.

Individual bricks, mortar, chipped surfaces and colour variation should
be added later through PBR textures.

However, the larger brick patterns that materially change the shape or
architectural rhythm should be represented.

⸻

5.  BRICK ARCHES

The ground-floor windows have distinctive shallow brick arches.

These must exist as real architectural geometry.

Do not make rectangular windows and rely on a future texture to fake the
arches.

Reconstruct:

curved top opening;

arch depth;

visible outer brick arch profile;

surrounding wall reveal.

Preserve the characteristic alternating red/darker brick rhythm only as
a future texture/material detail.

⸻

10. SECURITY GRILLES

These are visually important and should be actual geometry.

Model the metal mesh/grille using an efficient repeated grid.

Avoid modelling every wire with excessive radial subdivisions.

The grille should be visibly separate from the window behind it.

17. FILM DROP MECHANISM

The drop box should visually support the idea that an envelope can be
deposited.

If the actual slot or opening is visible in the references, model it.

If uncertain, keep the box externally accurate and provide a logical
interaction point without inventing an elaborate mechanical system.

Do not over-design unsupported details.

⸻

18. ENVELOPE + PENCIL HOLDER

21. GAMEPLAY WORKFLOW TO SUPPORT

The final game interaction may approximately work as:

PLAYER HAS EXPOSED FILM

↓

Player approaches CTL drop box.

↓

Interact.

↓

Interface references placing/confirming an order.

↓

Player takes or uses an envelope.

↓

Order number is associated with the film.

↓

Film is deposited into drop box.

↓

Development occurs after an in-game delay.

↓

Digital scans become available.

↓

Negatives may later be collected.

None of this logic should be built into Blender.

The Blender asset must only provide clean physical interaction points
and separable props.

⸻

22. DROP BOX AS A 24-HOUR ACCESS POINT

The drop box should remain usable in the game even when the main lab
entrance is considered closed.

Therefore:

it must be externally accessible;

it must not require the door to open;

collision around it should allow the player to stand directly in front;

interaction anchor should be independent from the main entrance.

SUCCESS CRITERION

The untextured building should already read as this specific historic
Manchester industrial facade through its brick arches, entrance
surround, window rhythm, grilles and proportions.

The Come Through Lab drop box must exist as a clearly separate hero
gameplay object mounted beside the entrance.

The envelope/pencil holder must also remain a separate interactable prop
system.

The final geometry should make it possible to reproduce the real CTL
film-drop process later without needing to remodel the facade.
