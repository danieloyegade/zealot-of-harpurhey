THE HIVE / ARTS COUNCIL ENGLAND

Untextured architectural reconstruction for Zealot of Harpurhey

Build an accurate, game-ready Blender reconstruction of The Hive on
Lever Street, Manchester, the building containing Arts Council England’s
Manchester office, using ALL supplied reference photographs.

This asset is for Zealot of Harpurhey, a Three.js game.

This task is ARCHITECTURAL GEOMETRY ONLY.

Do not create final textures, decals, signage graphics, weathering or
detailed materials yet.

The objective is to produce an architectural model that is recognisable
as The Hive from its massing, proportions, facade structure and geometry
alone, even when rendered entirely in neutral clay materials.

⸻

1.  IMPORTANT: STUDY ALL REFERENCES BEFORE MODELLING

Do not begin by modelling directly from the first image.

First inspect every supplied photograph and identify which architectural
information each image provides.

The references include:

a historical exterior three-quarter view showing the overall building
massing;

closer street-level views showing the black-brick lower volumes;

views clearly showing the large perforated metal facade/screens;

a near-frontal view of those screens;

a photograph of the current Lever Street entrance;

an interior architectural reference showing the character of the
lobby/reception area.

Cross-reference all images.

Do not reproduce photographic perspective distortion as building
geometry.

Infer a coherent rectilinear architectural structure from the different
viewpoints.

Before detailed modelling, establish:

approximate overall footprint;

street frontage length;

total height;

individual storey heights;

location and dimensions of the different building volumes;

relative position of the two taller pale-brick volumes;

lower black-brick volume;

screened central facade;

entrance locations;

setbacks;

window rhythm.

When exact dimensions cannot be determined, use conservative
architectural estimates based on doors, windows, floor heights, concrete
columns and other recognisable features.

Maintain consistency between elevations.

⸻

2.  MODEL THE BUILDING AS SEVERAL INTERLOCKING VOLUMES

Do not treat The Hive as one rectangular building with facade textures.

A defining characteristic of this architecture is its composition from
several overlapping volumes.

Establish these major masses first.

A. Ground/street-level volume

A relatively transparent base consisting of:

large glazed openings;

dark framing;

substantial pale concrete/stone structural columns;

recessed entrances;

shop/office-sized glazed bays;

sections recessed beneath upper floors.

B. Dark lower/middle building

Above and around the base is a substantial volume clad in very dark
charcoal/black brick.

This volume contains:

tall narrow windows;

vertically stacked window groups;

deeply contrasting dark masonry;

projecting and recessed sections;

corners that turn visibly around the building.

C. Screened central facade

A major portion of the Lever Street facade is covered by enormous
perforated metal screen panels mounted away from the building.

These screens are architecturally important and MUST NOT be represented
merely as a flat texture pasted onto the wall.

D. Pale upper towers

The building contains multiple taller upper volumes clad in pale
beige/tan brick.

These rise substantially above the dark lower structure.

They contain:

narrow vertical windows;

large dark vertical facade strips;

strong rectangular silhouettes;

recessed intermediate floors;

pronounced cantilevered/projecting upper masses.

The relationship between these towers and the lower building is one of
the most important aspects of the asset.

⸻

3.  MASSING FIRST

Construct a clean blockout before adding architectural detail.

The initial blockout should establish:

total building length;

approximate depth;

lower dark volume;

ground floor;

screened facade zone;

both major upper tower volumes;

setbacks;

cantilevers;

roof heights;

major corner conditions.

Do not proceed immediately into windows and facade detailing.

Create several clay/blockout renders first.

The building should already resemble The Hive based purely on its
silhouette.

⸻

4.  GROUND FLOOR

The ground floor should receive considerably more detail than distant
upper portions because the player will encounter the building at
pedestrian height.

Model:

structural concrete/stone columns;

recessed glazing;

full-height windows;

entrance openings;

door frames;

mullions;

deep facade recesses;

soffits/ceilings above recessed areas;

threshold geometry;

major wall divisions.

Windows should not simply sit on the wall plane.

Create believable:

reveals;

frame thickness;

glass position;

structural depth.

Use simple placeholder glass only.

⸻

5.  ARTS COUNCIL / 47-51 LEVER STREET ENTRANCE

THIS IS A HERO AREA OF THE MODEL.

The player will walk directly toward this entrance and attempt to
interact with it.

Therefore this portion should withstand much closer viewing than the
upper building.

Use the supplied contemporary photograph as the primary architectural
reference for the entrance.

Reconstruct:

large rectangular projecting canopy;

substantial dark side supports;

recessed entrance bay;

double entrance doors;

surrounding glazed panels;

door frames;

threshold;

facade behind the canopy;

depth between canopy and entrance;

neighbouring structural elements.

The entrance should feel physically recessed into the building.

Do not model the current printed graphics as geometry.

However, preserve clean surfaces suitable for later decals/textures.

The real-world photograph contains notices including:

“Entrance Closed”

and directions to use another entrance.

These will be recreated later as decals/materials.

Do not bake them into the architectural mesh.

⸻

6.  GAMEPLAY REQUIREMENT FOR THIS ENTRANCE

The Arts Council entrance has a specific gameplay function.

The player will approach the front entrance expecting to enter.

Therefore:

doors must be separately identifiable objects;

doorway/collision geometry must be clean;

entrance should support an interaction trigger;

the entrance must be easy to identify programmatically;

the doors should remain separate from surrounding facade geometry.

Suggested naming:

ACE_FrontEntrance

ACE_FrontDoor_Left

ACE_FrontDoor_Right

ACE_EntranceCanopy

ACE_EntranceGlass

ACE_EntranceTriggerAnchor

The trigger anchor can simply be an Empty placed logically in front of
the doors.

Do not implement game logic in Blender.

It only needs to provide a sensible anchor for Three.js.

⸻

7.  BLACK-BRICK FACADES

Model the dark brick portions as clean architectural surfaces.

Do not model individual bricks.

Brick relief will be handled through PBR materials later.

Concentrate instead on:

wall planes;

corners;

recesses;

window reveals;

changes in facade depth;

parapets;

junctions between black and pale sections.

Windows in these areas are predominantly narrow vertical openings.

Accurately reproduce their rhythm and relative dimensions.

Repeating windows may use linked/instanced geometry where appropriate.

⸻

8.  PERFORATED METAL SCREEN FACADE

This is one of the most distinctive architectural features of The Hive.

Large rectangular perforated metal panels are suspended in front of
portions of the facade.

These MUST have actual spatial separation from the building.

Model:

individual major panel sections;

surrounding frames;

vertical structural supports;

horizontal divisions where clearly visible;

mounting offset from the underlying facade.

The screens should visibly float/project in front of the building rather
than being coplanar with it.

However:

DO NOT model thousands of individual perforations.

That would be wasteful for a Three.js asset.

Instead create clean panel geometry that can later receive:

alpha/opacity texture;

normal map;

patterned material;

appropriate metallic shader.

The panel frames and major structural divisions should be geometry.

The microscopic mesh/perforation pattern should be texture/material
information.

The ornamental/figurative patterns visible within the screen should also
be handled later through textures/masks, not geometry.

⸻

9.  SCREEN DEPTH AND PARALLAX

Because players will walk along the pavement beside these screens, they
need believable parallax.

The architecture should consist approximately of:

building wall

→ windows / underlying facade

→ air gap

→ metal screen

→ street

The player should be able to perceive that the screen is a separate
architectural layer.

This is more important than modelling the tiny perforations.

⸻

10. INTERMEDIATE WHITE / RECESSED FLOOR

The reference photographs show lighter recessed facade sections between
the dark lower mass and pale upper towers.

Reconstruct these accurately.

Include:

white/light wall planes;

windows;

recess from the primary facade;

dark projecting shading elements/louvres;

structural relationship to the upper tower.

Several windows have large projecting dark louvre/shading structures.

These affect the silhouette and MUST be geometry.

Do not represent them with textures.

⸻

11. UPPER PALE-BRICK TOWERS

These should remain relatively efficient but architecturally accurate.

Pay particular attention to:

pale brick wall surfaces;

tall narrow windows;

dark vertical window/louvre strips;

corner window conditions;

deep window recesses;

roof/parapet line.

Do not model individual pale bricks.

Do not make every upper window uniquely high-poly.

Create efficient reusable window modules where dimensions repeat.

13. CORNERS

Carefully study the references for how windows and facade systems turn
corners.

Avoid the common procedural-building mistake where one facade simply
intersects another at 90 degrees without resolving the architectural
junction.

Properly construct:

brick corners;

glazing corners;

screen termination;

structural columns;

cantilever corners;

parapets.

⸻

14. INTERIOR: ENTRANCE / RECEPTION ONLY

The supplied interior photograph is a reference for the architectural
character of the interior.

Do NOT attempt to reconstruct the entire office building.

Create only enough interior architecture to support what a player might
see through glazing or encounter near the entrance.

Create a simplified:

lobby volume;

floor;

ceiling;

internal walls;

corridor suggestion;

reception area;

doorway/opening arrangement.

Where useful, loosely reference the supplied interior photograph:

pale concrete/block walls;

dark exposed ceiling;

long black ceiling beams/baffles;

recessed timber-framed openings;

dark reception desk;

minimal contemporary institutional architecture.

Do not model office workers, computers, chairs or detailed office
equipment at this stage.

Importantly, the player is not intended to freely explore the entire
Arts Council office in the initial implementation.

Therefore do not waste geometry constructing unseen floors.

15. GAMEPLAY INTERACTION CONCEPT

Do NOT implement dialogue or UI inside Blender.

However, construct the entrance so the later Three.js implementation can
support this sequence:

Player approaches front entrance.

Player attempts to enter.

Door does not open.

A rejection-style institutional message appears, approximately:

“Unfortunately, due to exceptionally high demand, you have not been
selected on this occasion.”

The architectural asset should therefore support the joke through
physical staging:

clearly visible entrance;

convincing doors;

player can walk directly up to them;

doors remain closed;

interaction point immediately outside.

Do not add exaggerated visual comedy to the architecture.

The building should remain realistic.

The humour comes from the interaction and the institutional language,
not from cartoon architecture.

18. REAL-WORLD SCALE

Work at real-world architectural scale.

1 Blender unit = 1 metre.

Z = vertical.

Ground level = Z 0.

Estimate scale using:

standard commercial doors;

people visible in references;

storey heights;

concrete columns;

window dimensions.

Do not arbitrarily scale the building after modelling.

Apply transforms before final export.

20. THREE.JS PERFORMANCE

This is a substantial building but will be viewed in real time.

Optimise intelligently.

Use geometry for:

silhouette;

major massing;

windows/recesses;

cornices;

structural columns;

screen frames;

facade depth;

large louvres;

entrance;

major projections.

Use future textures/materials for:

bricks;

metal perforations;

ornamental screen patterns;

concrete grain;

signage;

weathering;

tiny surface imperfections.

Reuse/instance repeating window geometry while constructing the asset
where practical.

Avoid excessive bevel segments.

Small bevels may be used on important architectural edges so they catch
light naturally.

Do not bevel everything uniformly.

Avoid the rounded, soft, plastic appearance common to poorly optimised
game architecture.

⸻

21. LEVEL OF DETAIL

Allocate geometry according to likely player distance.

HIGH DETAIL

Arts Council entrance, ground-floor columns, glazing, doors, screen
facade and street-level corners.

MEDIUM DETAIL

Black-brick lower floors, recessed intermediate level, large louvres,
lower tower windows.

LOWER DETAIL

Highest floors, roof elements and architectural surfaces unlikely to be
approached closely.

This does not mean altering their proportions. Their silhouettes and
window rhythms must remain accurate.

24. FINAL GEOMETRY VALIDATION

Before export, compare the model against every reference.

Specifically check:

Overall height-to-width ratio.

Relative height of dark and pale sections.

Tower dimensions.

Tower setbacks.

Cantilever depth.

Screen facade height and width.

Screen offset from building.

Ground-floor column spacing.

Window rhythm.

Entrance dimensions.

Entrance canopy depth.

Building corners.

Recessed intermediate floor.

Roof/parapet silhouette.

Look specifically for:

genericised architecture;

oversized frames;

toy-like proportions;

excessive bevels;

flat windows;

incorrect tower placement;

excessively thick screen structures.

Correct these before export.

SUCCESS CRITERION

The untextured model must be recognisable as The Hive before any brick
textures, metal textures, signage, Arts Council branding or
environmental detail are added.

If textures would be required to make the building recognisable, the
geometry is not yet sufficiently accurate.

Architectural fidelity takes priority over decorative detail.
