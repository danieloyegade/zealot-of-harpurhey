SPICE CABIN — HARpurhey / Manchester Takeaway Shopfront Codex / Blender
Asset Build Prompt Reference-led, reusable GLB asset for Zealot of
Harpurhey

1.  ROLE AND GOAL

You are building a production-ready Blender asset of the Spice Cabin
takeaway shopfront from the supplied reference photographs.

The goal is not to make a generic British takeaway inspired by Spice
Cabin. Reconstruct the actual architectural character visible in the
references as closely as practical, while keeping the asset efficient
enough for use in a Three.js game.

The finished asset should occupy the uncanny space between photographic
reality and a game environment: recognisably real, materially
convincing, slightly heightened, but not glossy, toy-like, Lego-like, or
over-clean.

Treat the supplied photographs as the primary visual authority.

Pay particular attention to: - the brown/tan brickwork and its coarse,
aged surface; - the horizontal half-log / rounded timber cladding across
the lower shopfront; - the large faux-wood Spice Cabin fascia sign; -
the deep blue structural fascia/border; - the blue-framed glazing and
doorway; - the cream/white structural pier at the left; - the black
bollards; - the security/barbed-wire silhouette along the roof edge; -
the utilitarian pipes, alarm boxes, brackets and small exterior
fixtures; - the slightly neglected pavement-level condition of the real
location.

Do not beautify the building.

2.  REFERENCE INTERPRETATION

Use all supplied reference images together rather than copying the
perspective of one photograph.

The references show a small end-unit takeaway beneath a shallow
commercial parade. The Spice Cabin frontage is bounded by a substantial
cream-painted vertical pier on the left and the neighbouring unit on the
right.

The upper wall is exposed tan/brown brick. A long timber-effect sign
panel sits across the upper shopfront. Beneath it is a saturated
dark/royal-blue fascia band. The shop glazing and door are also framed
in blue.

The lower frontage is unusually distinctive: instead of a conventional
flat panel, much of it is faced in horizontally stacked rounded timber
boards resembling half logs or log-cabin siding. This is a defining
visual feature and must be modelled and textured carefully.

The sign reads approximately: SPICE CABIN Pizza • Kebabs • Fried Chicken
• Burgers

The references show variations in the exact sign artwork over time.
Reproduce the recognisable Spice Cabin identity from the clearest
references rather than attempting to merge every historical variation
literally.

The shop should feel like a specific North Manchester neighbourhood
takeaway, not a polished chain restaurant.

3.  ARCHITECTURAL PROPORTIONS AND MASSING

Build the shopfront at plausible real-world scale.

Prioritise proportion matching: - relatively low, wide commercial
frontage; - upper exposed brick zone; - long rectangular signboard; -
thick blue horizontal fascia below the sign; - broad left display
window; - narrow central/right entrance; - lower timber cladding
occupying approximately the lower half of the glazed frontage; - cream
structural pier wrapping the left end of the unit; - neighbouring shop
boundary visible only where useful to establish the Spice Cabin edge.

Do not model an entire street unless necessary. The primary deliverable
is a modular shopfront/building-edge asset that can be placed into the
wider street scene.

Use real thickness where silhouette or shadow requires it. Avoid making
the facade a collection of completely flat planes.

4.  BRICK WALL — CRITICAL MATERIAL REQUIREMENT

The brick wall must receive unusually careful treatment.

Do NOT use: - a clean procedural brick shader; - uniformly coloured
orange bricks; - smooth plastic-looking brick; - perfectly identical
mortar joints; - an exaggerated displacement that looks like stone
blocks.

The reference brick is a muted buff/tan-to-brown commercial brick with
substantial age variation. Individual bricks have granular, slightly
pitted faces. Some are darker, some lighter, and the mortar is recessed
enough to catch narrow shadows.

Create the brick material using a physically plausible PBR approach: -
high-resolution albedo/base-colour texture; - roughness variation; -
normal/bump information for pores, chips and mortar depth; - subtle
displacement/parallax only if performance allows; - non-uniform brick
colour and weathering.

The brick faces should be predominantly matte with high roughness.
Introduce subtle staining and accumulated grime, especially: - beneath
roof-edge fixtures; - around pipework; - near the fascia; - around
corners; - close to the pavement.

Mortar should be desaturated grey-beige rather than bright white.

Avoid obvious texture repetition. Use sufficiently large texture
coverage, UV variation, decals, masks, or layered noise so the brick
does not reveal a repeating tile every few courses.

The brick pattern and scale must remain believable when the player
approaches the facade.

5.  LOG-CABIN TIMBER FACADE — CRITICAL MATERIAL AND GEOMETRY REQUIREMENT

The lower facade is one of the most important identifiers of the
building.

Do not represent it as a flat brown wall with horizontal lines.

Model the cladding as horizontal rounded timber/half-log courses with
genuine shallow cylindrical relief. Each course should project enough to
create a soft highlight along its upper curvature and a narrow
occlusion/shadow line between courses.

The timber is medium honey-brown to weathered golden brown, not orange
plastic.

Each log course should show: - subtle longitudinal wood grain; - small
colour differences between boards; - darker seams; - slightly worn or
dirty areas; - modest roughness variation; - occasional imperfections
rather than perfectly cloned pieces.

The surface should read as commercially installed faux-log/timber
cladding that has spent years outdoors.

Avoid: - glossy varnished wood; - perfectly smooth cylinders; -
identical procedural stripes; - cartoon wood knots; - exaggerated rustic
cabin styling.

The effect should be convincing from both medium distance and close
street-level inspection.

Where the timber meets the window frame, doorway and blue fascia,
include realistic trim, recesses and contact shadows. These junctions
are important for preventing the asset from looking like assembled toy
blocks.

6.  SPICE CABIN SIGNAGE

Create the large rectangular sign as its own mesh/material zone.

The sign background is a printed faux-horizontal-wood design, distinct
from the real rounded timber cladding below.

The main lettering should clearly read: SPICE CABIN

Reproduce the visual character: - “SPICE” predominantly red/white; -
“CABIN” predominantly green/white; - chilli/flame motifs where
visible; - bold takeaway-sign typography; - white/black edging and
dimensional-looking printed graphics; - smaller yellow/white
food-category line below.

Use a texture/decal for the graphic rather than attempting to construct
all typography as heavy 3D geometry.

The sign should not look freshly printed. Add extremely subtle outdoor
fading, grime and material roughness, while preserving legibility.

If the phone number is included, use the reference-visible number where
legible. Do not invent additional advertising copy.

7.  BLUE FASCIA, WINDOW AND DOOR FRAMES

The deep blue elements are visually important.

Use a saturated but slightly weathered commercial blue. It should not
look like pristine glossy automotive paint.

Include: - horizontal blue band immediately below the main sign; -
vertical blue framing around glazing; - blue trim around the entrance; -
neighbouring blue structural continuation only where required.

Material characteristics: - painted metal/timber appearance; - moderate
roughness; - subtle scratches, faded patches and accumulated dirt; -
darker grime in corners and under overhangs.

The glazing should remain physically plausible and relatively dark from
outside. Avoid mirror-like glass.

The interior may be represented efficiently, but the shop should not
appear as an empty transparent box. Use dark interior geometry/cards and
restrained warm illumination where appropriate.

8.  WINDOWS, DOOR AND INTERIOR READ

Construct the front window and entrance with actual recess depth.

The entrance is narrow and visually darker than the surrounding facade.
The timber cladding continues around/below the glazing.

Include the characteristic illuminated takeaway-window detail where
appropriate, such as a small red “FRIED CHICKEN” style sign visible in
the references.

Keep interior detail economical: - dark counter/kitchen silhouettes; -
warm practical light; - a few broad forms behind the glass; - no need to
fully model the restaurant unless this later becomes an enterable
interior.

The facade should still work convincingly at night, because the game’s
visual language relies heavily on nocturnal urban illumination.

9.  STRUCTURAL AND STREET DETAILS

Model the details that make this location specific rather than generic.

Include where visible: - cream/white painted structural pier at the
left; - weathering, chips and dirt on that pier; - black cylindrical
bollards in front of the shop; - metal pedestrian railing near the left
edge if the asset scope includes immediate pavement furniture; -
black/grey rainwater pipework near the neighbouring-unit boundary; -
alarm/security boxes; - exterior brackets; - long pale pipe/conduit
running high across the brick wall; - roof-edge anti-climb/barbed-wire
assembly.

The roof-edge security wire is visually important to the silhouette but
should be performance-conscious. Use simplified geometry and/or
alpha-card techniques rather than unnecessarily dense modelling.

Do not make every accessory pristine or perfectly aligned.

10. PAVEMENT AND GROUND CONTACT

If including a pavement module, match the ordinary worn Manchester
streetscape rather than producing a clean architectural visualisation.

Use: - dark grey paving/asphalt; - patched and uneven tonal areas; -
shallow puddle/damp variation where appropriate; - grime around wall
edges; - small accumulated debris decals used sparingly; - convincing
contact darkening around bollards and facade edges.

Do not permanently model large piles of litter into the base asset
unless they are separate toggleable props.

The building must feel physically seated in the pavement. Avoid floating
geometry or unnaturally clean base edges.

11. WEATHERING AND AGE

The reference location contains years of ordinary urban wear. Preserve
that.

Weathering should be layered and restrained: - grime under ledges; -
darker rain streaks; - paint wear; - small chips; - roughness
variation; - dirt around door thresholds; - discoloration around
fixtures; - subtle moss/organic staining only where plausible.

Do not use generic “grunge” uniformly across every material. Weathering
should follow gravity, touch, water flow and material boundaries.

The target is observed realism, not a post-apocalyptic texture pass.

12. GAME AESTHETIC

The asset belongs to Zealot of Harpurhey.

The desired visual language is no longer overtly Dreamcast or
deliberately low-poly. It should sit between hyper-real and game-like.

Aim for: - realistic materials; - strong recognisable geometry; -
photographic specificity; - controlled simplification; - slightly
uncanny stillness; - clean enough forms to read as a designed game
world; - enough material imperfection to resist the “plastic Blender
asset” appearance.

Do not stylise away the banal details. The ordinary takeaway
architecture is part of the artistic value.

At night, the sign, glazing, blue trim, bollards, brick texture and
security-wire silhouette should create a strong planimetric urban
composition suitable for the broader The Spectres Are All Around Us
visual language.

13. THREE.JS / GLB PERFORMANCE

Build for practical real-time use.

Requirements: - apply transforms; - sensible origin placement; -
real-world scale; - clean normals; - no unnecessary hidden geometry; -
no accidental duplicate faces; - efficient topology; - UVs packed
logically; - PBR textures suitable for glTF; - minimise material count
where this does not compromise fidelity; - use texture atlases where
sensible; - use instancing/repeated geometry for repeated timber courses
or fixtures when useful; - use LOD strategy if high-detail brick/timber
geometry becomes expensive.

Do not model individual bricks across the entire wall. The brick depth
should primarily come from normal/bump/displacement techniques.

The timber cladding may use repeated low-segment rounded geometry
because its silhouette and grazing-light response are important.

Keep transparent materials to a minimum.

14. MODULARITY

Separate logical components so they can be adjusted or replaced later.

Recommended object groups: SPICE_structure SPICE_brick_wall
SPICE_cream_pier SPICE_sign SPICE_blue_fascia SPICE_window_frames
SPICE_glass SPICE_door SPICE_log_cladding SPICE_bollards
SPICE_security_wire SPICE_pipework SPICE_alarm_fixtures
SPICE_interior_cards SPICE_pavement_optional

Do not unnecessarily merge everything into one mesh before export.

15. MATERIAL SET

Use a concise but convincing material library, approximately:
MAT_brick_aged_tan MAT_mortar_integrated MAT_log_cladding_weathered
MAT_sign_printed_wood MAT_blue_painted_frame MAT_cream_painted_concrete
MAT_glass_shopfront MAT_black_bollard MAT_dark_pipe MAT_security_metal
MAT_pavement_worn MAT_interior_dark MAT_emissive_signage

Materials should be glTF-compatible.

16. LIGHTING / VALIDATION RENDERS

Create validation renders before final export.

Render at least: A. neutral overcast daylight; B. oblique
daylight/grazing light to reveal brick and timber relief; C. night
version with restrained shop/interior practical illumination; D. close
crop of brick wall; E. close crop of rounded timber facade; F.
straight-on facade view.

The close-up tests are mandatory.

If the brick reads as a flat texture, revise it. If the timber reads as
smooth plastic tubes or Lego, revise it. If the blue framing looks
pristine and synthetic, revise it. If the whole shop looks like a
generic takeaway rather than the supplied Spice Cabin, revise it.

17. QUALITY CHECKLIST

Before export verify:

[ ] Overall silhouette matches the references. [ ] Shop width/height
relationships feel correct. [ ] Cream left pier is present. [ ] Brick
colour is muted tan/brown rather than bright orange. [ ] Brick has
granular roughness, mortar depth and non-repeating variation. [ ] Brick
remains convincing at close player distance. [ ] Rounded horizontal log
cladding is actual relief, not painted stripes. [ ] Timber grain follows
the horizontal boards. [ ] Timber has weathering and roughness
variation. [ ] Timber does not look glossy, plastic, or Lego-like. [ ]
Faux-wood sign background is distinct from the real timber cladding. [ ]
SPICE CABIN lettering is recognisable and correctly positioned. [ ] Blue
fascia and framing proportions match the references. [ ] Door/window
recesses create convincing depth. [ ] Exterior pipes/security fixtures
are represented. [ ] Roof-edge anti-climb/barbed-wire silhouette is
present. [ ] Bollards are correctly scaled. [ ] Materials respond
naturally under both daylight and night lighting. [ ] No obvious texture
tiling. [ ] No unnecessary geometry. [ ] GLB imports correctly into a
Three.js test scene.

18. EXPORT

Export a production GLB suitable for the existing game pipeline.

Use: - glTF 2.0 / .glb; - embedded or correctly packed textures; -
applied scale/rotation; - +Y / axis handling appropriate to the
project’s established Blender-to-Three.js pipeline; - sensible object
names; - no cameras or temporary validation lights unless specifically
required by the existing pipeline.

Preserve enough object separation for later material tuning and
interaction.

FINAL PRIORITY ORDER

When compromises are required, prioritise in this order:

1.  Recognisable Spice Cabin proportions and silhouette.
2.  Convincing brick texture and scale.
3.  Convincing rounded log-cladding geometry and material.
4.  Signage and blue shopfront identity.
5.  Architectural depth around windows/door/fascia.
6.  Specific exterior fixtures and weathering.
7.  Interior suggestion.
8.  Minor street clutter.

The finished asset should look like the actual Spice Cabin has been
carefully reconstructed for an uncanny, photographically grounded
Manchester game world, not like a generic takeaway asset with a Spice
Cabin sign pasted onto it.
