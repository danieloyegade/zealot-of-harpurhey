ADVANCED PHOTO

ADVANCED PHOTO, ST ANN’S ARCADE, MANCHESTER

Untextured Blender reconstruction for Zealot of Harperhey

Create an accurate, game-ready Blender reconstruction of Advanced Photo,
the photographic/film-processing shop located inside the arcade by St
Ann’s Square in Manchester, using ALL supplied reference photographs.

This asset is for Zealot of Harperhey, a Three.js game.

IMPORTANT: THIS IS A GEOMETRY-ONLY PASS

Do not create final textures.

Do not create:

photographic textures;

logos;

Advanced Photo graphics;

posters;

product packaging;

film-brand labels;

advertisements;

decals;

dirt/weathering;

final PBR materials.

The purpose of this pass is to reconstruct the physical architecture,
shopfront, display cabinets and major interior fixtures.

The finished asset should already be recognisable as this specific shop
when rendered using plain clay/placeholder materials.

⸻

1.  STUDY ALL REFERENCE IMAGES FIRST

Before modelling, inspect all supplied photographs together.

They show the shop from several useful viewpoints:

frontal views of the main display window;

oblique views showing the depth of the shop;

wider views showing how the frontage wraps around the arcade corner;

views showing the entrance;

views showing the large glazed display cases;

views showing the interior counter and cabinetry;

views showing the relationship between Advanced Photo and the
surrounding arcade.

Do not treat any single photograph as a perfect orthographic reference.

Cross-reference them to infer the actual three-dimensional arrangement.

Correct for photographic perspective rather than reproducing lens
distortion as geometry.

⸻

2.  SCOPE

This asset should include:

A. Advanced Photo shopfront

B. Entrance

C. Main glazed display windows

D. Basic shop interior

E. Major display cabinets

F. Main counter

G. Enough surrounding arcade architecture to make the shop physically
coherent

Do NOT attempt to reconstruct the entire arcade.

A short architectural envelope immediately surrounding the shop is
sufficient for later connection to the larger environment.

⸻

3.  OVERALL SHOP LAYOUT

Reconstruct the shop as a relatively small photographic retailer
embedded into the arcade.

The shop has a distinctive black-framed traditional shopfront wrapped
around a corner/angled frontage.

Establish the actual floor plan before modelling detail.

Pay attention to:

main entrance position;

large front display window;

secondary display window;

corner relationship between the two glazed facades;

shop depth;

interior circulation;

counter position;

wall-mounted display areas.

Do not simplify the entire shop into a rectangular room with a glass
wall.

The geometry of the wrapping shopfront is important.

⸻

4.  BLACK ARCHITECTURAL SHOPFRONT

This is the most recognisable architectural feature.

Reconstruct the black painted shopfront system accurately.

Model:

substantial black vertical framing;

black horizontal fascia;

projecting/recessed moulding;

window surrounds;

doorway surround;

base/plinth beneath display windows;

rectangular decorative panels along the lower frontage;

corner transitions;

upper framing;

structural divisions between glass panels.

Do NOT make this a single flat black rectangle.

The reference photographs clearly show multiple layers of moulding and
projection.

These should have actual depth.

Use modest bevels on exposed edges so the geometry catches light
naturally.

Avoid exaggerated bevels that make the shopfront look plastic or
toy-like.

⸻

5.  MAIN DISPLAY WINDOW

The large front photographic display is a HERO AREA.

The player will see this at close range.

Reconstruct:

large outer glass window;

black structural surround;

deep lower plinth;

decorative rectangular panels beneath;

internal glass display cabinet;

glass shelves;

vertical supports;

display base;

rear/internal glazing where visible.

Importantly, the display window has depth.

It should not consist of products pasted behind a single glass plane.

Build an actual shallow display volume behind the external glazing.

⸻

6.  GLASS DISPLAY CABINETS

Advanced Photo contains multiple glass cabinets used to display cameras,
film and photographic equipment.

Model the cabinets themselves.

Include:

glass outer panels;

thin metal/frame structures;

glass shelves;

cabinet bases;

cabinet tops;

doors/panel divisions where visible.

Keep these efficient.

Do NOT populate them with hundreds of products during this pass.

Products will later be modular prop assets.

However, the shelves must physically exist so those products can later
be placed correctly.

Suggested objects:

AP_DisplayCabinet_Front

AP_DisplayCabinet_Interior01

AP_DisplayCabinet_Interior02

AP_GlassShelves

⸻

7.  ENTRANCE

Reconstruct the shop entrance accurately.

Model:

doorway opening;

black frame;

door;

glass panel;

handle where geometrically significant;

threshold;

surrounding framing;

relationship between doorway and adjacent display window.

The entrance must be usable by the player.

Maintain realistic human scale.

Keep the door as a separate object.

Suggested naming:

AP_Entrance

AP_Door

AP_DoorFrame

AP_EntranceTriggerAnchor

Place an Empty named AP_EntranceTriggerAnchor near the entrance for
later Three.js interaction logic.

Do not implement interaction logic in Blender.

⸻

8.  INTERIOR

Unlike a background storefront, Advanced Photo is intended to function
as an enterable game location.

Therefore construct a real, simplified interior.

Model:

floor;

ceiling;

walls;

structural columns where visible;

counter;

major cabinetry;

major shelving;

glass display cases;

rear work/service area suggestion;

doorway/openings where visible.

Do not attempt to recreate every object visible in the photographs.

Concentrate on the permanent architecture and furniture.

⸻

9.  MAIN COUNTER

Create the customer service / photographic processing counter as a
separate modular object.

Use the references to approximate:

counter length;

counter height;

depth;

customer-facing side;

staff working side;

storage volume underneath.

Keep enough clear space behind the counter for an NPC shopkeeper.

Create an Empty:

AP_ShopkeeperAnchor

positioned behind the counter at a plausible standing location.

Also create:

AP_CustomerInteractionAnchor

immediately in front of the counter.

These will later support dialogue and purchasing/film-processing
interactions in Three.js.

⸻

10. FILM-DEVELOPMENT GAMEPLAY REQUIREMENTS

This shop will function as a photographic location within the game.

The player may bring exposed 35mm film here for development and/or
purchase photographic supplies.

Therefore design the geometry to support:

→

PLAYER APPROACHES COUNTER

→

SHOPKEEPER INTERACTION

→

FILM / DEVELOPMENT TRANSACTION

Only ensure that:

floor space accommodates player movement;

counter is accessible;

NPC position is logical;

cabinets do not obstruct circulation;

interaction anchors exist.

⸻

11. PRODUCT DISPLAY STRATEGY

Do NOT individually model everything visible in the photographs.

The references contain many:

cameras;

film boxes;

batteries;

photographic accessories;

photo frames;

passport-photo advertisements;

memory cards;

small electronics.

These will eventually be separate reusable prop assets.

During this geometry stage, create only a very small number of neutral
primitive placeholder objects if necessary to judge shelf spacing.

Remove those placeholders before final GLB export unless specifically
required.

The important thing is that the display architecture is correctly
constructed.

⸻

12. WALL DISPLAY AREAS

Create simplified physical structures for the interior wall displays
visible in the references.

Model:

wall-mounted shelving;

backing panels;

display rails where substantial;

cabinet volumes;

major recessed/display zones.

Do not model individual advertisements or packaging.

⸻

13. CEILING

Reconstruct the visible ceiling volume sufficiently for an enterable
interior.

Include:

main ceiling plane;

major changes in ceiling height;

obvious bulkheads;

significant recessed areas.

Do not model every spotlight as high-poly geometry.

Simple fixture placeholders may be created where needed for later
lighting placement.

⸻

14. LIGHTING ANCHORS

The eventual shop should feel brightly and somewhat densely illuminated.

Do NOT create final lighting during this modelling pass.

However, create logically named Empty objects where useful:

AP_Light_Ceiling01

AP_Light_Ceiling02

AP_Light_Display01

AP_Light_Display02

These can later guide Three.js lighting.

Do not create dozens of unnecessary light anchors.

⸻

15. SURROUNDING ARCADE

Model only enough surrounding arcade architecture to contextualise the
shop.

Include approximately:

immediate floor outside the shop;

short ceiling section;

immediate neighbouring wall;

arcade-facing shopfront boundary;

any structural column directly attached to Advanced Photo.

The wider arcade will eventually be a separate environment asset.

Make the boundary clean enough that Advanced Photo can later connect to
it without visible gaps.

⸻

16. GLASS

There is a lot of glazing in this asset.

Separate major glass surfaces logically.

Suggested objects:

AP_ExteriorGlass

AP_DisplayGlass

AP_CabinetGlass

AP_DoorGlass

Do not create thick multi-layer physically simulated glass.

Use efficient planar or minimally thick geometry suitable for real-time
rendering.

Ensure overlapping glass surfaces are kept to a sensible minimum because
excessive transparency can become expensive and visually problematic in
Three.js.

20. REAL-WORLD SCALE

1 Blender unit = 1 metre

Z = vertical

Floor = Z 0

Use standard door dimensions, cabinet heights and other recognisable
architectural objects to infer scale.

The shop should feel relatively compact.

Do not enlarge the interior merely to make player navigation easier.

Preserve believable real-world dimensions first.

If gameplay navigation later requires adjustment, that can be handled
separately.

22. THREE.JS OPTIMISATION

This is a close-range enterable asset, but it still needs to run
efficiently in Three.js.

Prioritise polygons for:

shopfront silhouette;

moulding;

recesses;

doorway;

display cases;

counter;

shelves;

architectural depth.

Do not spend polygons on:

tiny product details;

microscopic trim;

lettering;

screws;

surface imperfections.

Reuse identical shelf components where possible.

Avoid excessive bevel segments.

Avoid unnecessary subdivisions.

Avoid hidden duplicate geometry.

Be particularly careful with transparent glass geometry.

⸻

23. BLOCKOUT FIRST

Do not immediately create the detailed model.

First create:

advanced_photo_blockout.blend

The blockout should establish only:

shop footprint;

arcade relationship;

entrance;

shopfront angle/corner;

primary display windows;

interior dimensions;

counter;

major display cabinets.

Then create four clay renders:

VIEW A

Straight-on view corresponding approximately to the main frontal
reference photograph.

VIEW B

Oblique arcade view corresponding approximately to the photograph
showing the circular ceiling light.

VIEW C

Looking through the entrance into the shop.

VIEW D

Interior/player-eye view looking back toward the shopfront.

STOP AFTER THE BLOCKOUT AND PRESENT THESE VIEWS FOR REVIEW.

Do not proceed automatically to detailed geometry.

⸻

25. FINAL VALIDATION

Compare the final clay model against every reference photograph.

Check:

Shop width.

Shop depth.

Entrance width and position.

Window proportions.

Corner/wraparound geometry.

Fascia height.

Black frame thickness.

Display-case depth.

Cabinet heights.

Counter position.

Interior circulation.

Arcade connection.

Specifically look for the common failure mode where the asset becomes
too chunky, rounded, simplified or toy-like.

The references show relatively thin, elegant shopfront framing and large
expanses of glass.

Preserve that delicacy.

SUCCESS CRITERION

The untextured clay model should already clearly read as Advanced Photo
from the supplied references.

Do not use textures, signs, products or logos to disguise inaccurate
architecture.

The model should feel like a small real photographic shop inside a
Manchester arcade, not a generic game shop.
