SALFORD CELLULOID NIGHT VIGNETTE

Modular untextured asset reconstruction for Zealot of Harperhey

Use the supplied still from the short film Salford Celluloid as the
primary visual reference.

This scene must NOT be constructed as one fused Blender model.

Instead, reconstruct it as a collection of independent, reusable game
assets that can be arranged together to reproduce the original cinematic
composition.

This is for Zealot of Harperhey, rendered in Three.js.

This task is primarily GEOMETRY ONLY.

Do not create final photographic textures, skin materials, clothing
textures, decals, dirt, final lighting, or final shaders yet.

The final scene should be reconstructible from the following independent
objects:

1.  Car

2.  Woman sitting/leaning on car

3.  Woman inside car

4.  Saxophonist with saxophone

5.  Saxophone case

6.  Litter bin

7.  Telephone booth

8.  Orange roadworks barrier

9.  Brick wall / facade behind telephone booth

10. Streetlight

11. Telephone-booth interior light

DO NOT model the overhead ventilation/ducting visible high in the
original film still.

⸻

1.  CORE PRINCIPLE: EVERYTHING SEPARATE

Every important scene element must remain independently movable.

Do NOT:

-   join characters to the car;

-   join the saxophonist to the road barrier;

-   join the saxophone case to the pavement;

-   join the telephone booth to the brick wall;

-   join the streetlight to the wall;

-   fuse lighting geometry into other objects.

The scene should be assembled from modular assets.

Suggested top-level collection:

SALFORD_CELLULOID_SCENE

containing:

SC_Car

SC_Woman_OnCar

SC_Woman_InCar

SC_Saxophonist

SC_SaxophoneCase

SC_Bin

SC_PhoneBooth

SC_RoadBarrier

SC_BrickWall

SC_Streetlight

SC_LightAnchors

⸻

2.  REFERENCE SCENE

After creating the individual assets, assemble a reference scene
reproducing the original film still as closely as possible.

Use the reference for:

-   relative scale;

-   spatial relationships;

-   camera position;

-   object placement;

-   character posing;

-   distance between objects.

The reference scene is for composition only.

All components must remain independently editable.

⸻

3.  CAR

Create the silver/grey compact hatchback shown in the reference.

Treat the car as a reusable hero prop.

Model:

-   body shell;

-   bonnet;

-   roof;

-   doors;

-   windows;

-   windscreen;

-   rear-view mirrors;

-   wheel arches;

-   four wheels;

-   tyres;

-   headlights;

-   basic interior visible through windows;

-   front seats;

-   dashboard silhouette;

-   steering wheel if visible.

Do not create a completely generic rectangular hatchback.

Use the reference to reproduce the overall proportions and early-2000s
compact-car silhouette.

Do not obsess over microscopic manufacturer-specific mechanical details
unless clearly visible.

Keep:

SC_Car_Body

SC_Car_Wheels

SC_Car_Glass

SC_Car_Interior

logically separated.

⸻

4.  CAR INTERACTION / GAME USE

The car may later function as a broken-down environmental object.

Therefore:

-   doors should be separable if practical;

-   driver’s/passenger windows should be distinct glass objects;

-   bonnet may optionally remain separate;

-   passenger compartment must have believable depth.

The car should not contain baked character geometry.

⸻

5.  WOMAN LEANING / SITTING ON CAR

Create the woman in front of the car as a completely separate character
asset:

SC_Woman_OnCar

The pose should match the film still:

-   body leaning backward against/front of car;

-   legs extended toward ground;

-   torso tilted backward;

-   head lifted upward;

-   relaxed but theatrical posture;

-   arms held relatively close behind/downward depending on the
    reference.

This character is part of the film vignette and should read as a posed
human figure rather than furniture fused to the bonnet.

⸻

6.  CHARACTER CONSTRUCTION

For this pass, prioritise:

-   believable human proportions;

-   silhouette;

-   pose;

-   hair volume;

-   clothing silhouette.

Do NOT concentrate on:

-   skin pores;

-   facial texture;

-   detailed fabric;

-   individual hair strands.

Ideally create or preserve a simple armature:

SC_Woman_OnCar_Rig

so the pose can later be changed or subtly animated.

If rigging is outside the current modelling scope, keep the mesh
separate and anatomically sensible for later rigging.

⸻

7.  WOMAN INSIDE CAR

Create the seated woman inside the car as another separate character:

SC_Woman_InCar

Do not merge her with the vehicle.

Match the visible pose:

-   seated in front passenger/driver area according to reference;

-   upper torso visible through side window;

-   one arm raised toward face/window;

-   seated posture matching car interior.

Where the photograph provides insufficient detail below window level,
infer the simplest plausible seated pose.

Do not invent elaborate details that cannot be seen.

Ideally preserve:

SC_Woman_InCar_Rig

for later animation.

⸻

8.  CHARACTER / CAR RELATIONSHIP

Both women should use the car only as a positional reference.

Create Empties:

SC_CarAnchor_WomanOutside

SC_CarAnchor_WomanInside

The character assets can later be snapped/aligned to those positions.

This is preferable to parenting their mesh permanently into the car GLB.

⸻

9.  SAXOPHONIST

Create the standing saxophonist as:

SC_Saxophonist

The saxophone can remain associated with the saxophonist for the default
pose, but it should preferably remain its own mesh/object:

SC_Saxophone

This makes later animation much easier.

Match the reference:

-   standing near the roadworks barriers;

-   instrument held in playing position;

-   head/torso inclined toward saxophone;

-   relaxed street-performance stance.

Preserve believable proportions.

⸻

10. SAXOPHONE

Model the saxophone with enough detail to read clearly at gameplay
distance.

Include:

-   curved main body;

-   bell;

-   neck;

-   mouthpiece;

-   major key clusters as simplified forms.

Do not model hundreds of tiny rods and screws.

The silhouette and brass-instrument form matter most.

Suggested:

SC_Saxophone_Body

SC_Saxophone_Mouthpiece

⸻

11. SAXOPHONE CASE

The saxophone case is a separate asset:

SC_SaxophoneCase

Reconstruct the open/partially open case visible near the musician.

Model:

-   outer shell;

-   lid;

-   hinge relationship;

-   interior cavity;

-   handle.

Keep the lid and base as separate objects if practical.

This should be independently movable.

⸻

12. LITTER BIN

Create the cylindrical public litter bin visible near the phone booth:

SC_Bin

Reconstruct:

-   cylindrical body;

-   domed/sloped upper rim if visible;

-   opening;

-   base;

-   slight taper where appropriate.

Keep it simple but recognisable.

Do not bake dirt/graffiti into geometry.

⸻

13. ORANGE ROADWORKS BARRIER

Create the plastic roadworks barrier as:

SC_RoadBarrier

It must be separate from the saxophonist.

Model:

-   rectangular plastic frame;

-   feet;

-   vertical end structures;

-   internal horizontal/vertical pattern;

-   connection points.

Do not create an excessively high-poly plastic mesh.

The orange colour and reflective markings will be added later.

If multiple barriers are visible, model one reusable barrier and
instance it.

⸻

14. TELEPHONE BOOTH

The booth is a major hero environmental prop.

Create:

SC_PHONE_BOOTH_MASTER

This must be a real enterable volume.

The player should eventually be able to walk inside it.

Model:

-   outer enclosure;

-   door/opening;

-   glass/transparent panels where applicable;

-   roof;

-   floor;

-   internal back wall;

-   telephone equipment housing;

-   shelf/panel;

-   major signage or information panels as blank decal surfaces.

Do not make it a shallow facade.

⸻

15. PHONE BOOTH ACCESS

Maintain adequate realistic interior space for a human player.

The booth should support:

-   entering;

-   turning slightly;

-   looking at internal phone equipment;

-   interacting with something inside.

Do not artificially enlarge it excessively, but ensure collision can
function reasonably.

Keep the door/opening separate if it may later move.

Suggested:

SC_PhoneBooth_Shell

SC_PhoneBooth_Door

SC_PhoneBooth_Glass

SC_PhoneBooth_Phone

SC_PhoneBooth_InteractAnchor

⸻

16. PHONE EQUIPMENT

The central/internal phone area visible in the reference should be
reconstructed in simplified form.

Model:

-   telephone housing;

-   handset shape;

-   keypad/control region;

-   major information panel;

-   lower service panel.

Do not attempt readable printed text yet.

Use decal surfaces for later signage.

⸻

17. PHONE BOOTH INTERIOR LIGHT

The booth has an important cold interior light.

Do NOT bake this into the geometry.

Create:

SC_PhoneBooth_InteriorLightAnchor

at the appropriate upper/internal position.

Later the light should approximately produce:

-   cool blue/cyan illumination;

-   strong illumination on the internal back panel;

-   cooler colour than the surrounding streetlight;

-   subtle spill through the entrance/glass.

⸻

18. BRICK WALL BEHIND PHONE BOOTH

Create the brick wall / low building facade as a separate architecture
asset:

SC_BrickWall

Reconstruct:

-   wall dimensions;

-   actual wall thickness;

-   phone-booth recess relationship;

-   upper edge/parapet;

-   corner return visible at right;

-   major doorway/opening recesses if visible.

Do not model individual bricks.

Use geometry for:

-   wall shape;

-   corners;

-   large recesses;

-   sill/edge details.

Use later PBR materials for brick and mortar.

⸻

19. IMPORTANT WALL RULE

Do not project the original film still onto the brick wall.

The wall must be a real three-dimensional architectural surface.

Do not embed people, car reflections, lighting or props into the wall
texture during this stage.

⸻

20. STREETLIGHT

Create the streetlight visible above/near the saxophonist:

SC_Streetlight

Reconstruct:

-   vertical pole;

-   base;

-   lamp head;

-   mounting geometry.

Match the approximate height and proportions from the reference.

Do not fuse the streetlight into the pavement or barrier.

⸻

21. STREETLIGHT LIGHT SOURCE

Add an Empty:

SC_Streetlight_LightAnchor

positioned inside/below the lamp head.

This will later become the dominant warm light for the scene.

The final lighting should approximate the still:

-   warm yellow/green sodium-like cast;

-   broad illumination across car and characters;

-   warm pool around saxophonist;

-   contrast against cold blue phone-booth light.

Do not bake this lighting into textures.

⸻

22. LIGHTING CONTRAST IS IMPORTANT

The eventual vignette should rely on two distinct lighting families:

WARM STREET LIGHT

from the lamp above the saxophonist.

COLD PHONE BOOTH LIGHT

from inside the booth.

These should remain separate in the Three.js scene.

The car and characters should receive real-time lighting rather than
pre-baked colour.

⸻

23. DO NOT INCLUDE OVERHEAD VENTS

The original studio-set image contains visible overhead
ventilation/ductwork.

DO NOT model this.

It is not wanted as part of the reusable vignette.

The game reconstruction should preserve the street-level scene without
reproducing every studio-set element.

⸻

24. GROUND

Create only a simple temporary ground plane for the reference
composition.

If a permanent environment patch is desired, keep it separately named:

SC_GroundPatch

Do not merge props into the ground.

Later surface textures can reproduce:

-   wet/dark tarmac;

-   pavement;

-   road markings;

-   grime.

⸻

25. SCENE AUDIO ANCHOR

The original short-film scene features the woman reciting Rilke’s First
Elegy after her car breaks down.

The original film audio should eventually be reusable in the game
vignette.

Do not embed audio in Blender geometry.

Create an Empty:

SC_RilkeAudioAnchor

positioned near the woman outside the car.

This can later be used by Three.js as the origin for spatial or
semi-spatial playback.

The actual audio implementation belongs to the game engine.

⸻

26. SAXOPHONE AUDIO ANCHOR

Also create:

SC_SaxophoneAudioAnchor

at the saxophonist’s approximate position.

This gives later sound design the option to spatialise:

-   live saxophone;

-   ambient music;

-   diegetic performance audio.

Do not implement audio in Blender.

⸻

27. CHARACTER AUDIO / ANIMATION COMPATIBILITY

Do not fuse:

-   mouth;

-   hands;

-   instrument;

-   car;

together in ways that make later animation impossible.

The eventual vignette may include subtle:

-   head movement;

-   breathing;

-   saxophone playing;

-   hand movement;

-   woman speaking/reciting.

Build with that future possibility in mind.

29. REAL-WORLD SCALE

1 Blender unit = 1 metre

Z = vertical

Use human figures and the car as primary scale references.

Maintain consistent scale across all assets.

This is especially important because they need to recombine into the
reference composition correctly.

30. ORIGINS

-   car origin near centre of vehicle at ground level;

-   characters at feet/root;

-   saxophone case near base centre;

-   bin at bottom centre;

-   phone booth at ground centre;

-   barrier at centre of base;

-   wall at ground/base corner;

-   streetlight at base of pole.

Correct origins are important for later Three.js placement.

SUCCESS CRITERION

The completed asset set should allow the original Salford Celluloid
frame to be reconstructed in 3D, while also allowing every significant
object to be reused, moved or removed independently.

Do not reconstruct the photograph as one static diorama. Reconstruct the
physical ingredients that created the photograph.
