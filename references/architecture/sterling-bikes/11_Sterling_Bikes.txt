STERLING BIKES

Shared e-bike + docking station asset system for Zealot of Harpurhey

Create a modular, game-ready Blender asset system for Sterling Bikes,
the fictional public shared-bike service in Zealot of Harpurhey.

Sterling Bikes is visually inspired by the yellow Manchester shared
e-bikes shown in the supplied references.

The name STERLING is an intentional fictional variation.

The finished game design should strongly evoke the real shared-bike
system through:

-   yellow step-through e-bike frame;

-   heavy-duty shared-bike construction;

-   partially enclosed rear wheel;

-   front basket;

-   integrated lights;

-   broad tyres;

-   upright handlebars;

-   chain guard;

-   docking hardware;

-   distinctive street-bike silhouette;

while using STERLING BIKES as the final in-game branding.

This task is primarily GEOMETRY ONLY.

Do not create final branding textures, decals, dirt, stickers,
scratches, final lighting or PBR materials yet.

3.  BIKE OVERALL PROPORTIONS

Reconstruct the bicycle as a believable heavy-duty urban rental e-bike.

It should have:

-   low step-through frame;

-   upright riding position;

-   relatively small/medium wheels;

-   broad durable tyres;

-   heavy frame tubing;

-   integrated electronic/battery section;

-   rear wheel cover;

-   front basket;

-   full mudguards;

-   durable pedals;

-   utilitarian saddle.

It should NOT resemble:

-   a lightweight road bike;

-   a mountain bike;

-   a BMX;

-   a generic bicycle with a basket attached.

The recognisable public-bike silhouette is important.

SUCCESS CRITERION

The bicycle should immediately read as a Manchester-style public shared
e-bike even without branding or textures.

It should be a fully reusable vehicle asset rather than a static prop.

The docking station should operate as a modular system in which:

-   bikes can appear;

-   bikes can disappear;

-   empty docks still look correct;

-   one bike asset can be reused across the entire game.

The final fictional version should be recognisable as:

STERLING BIKES

rather than Starling Bikes.

There are a couple of choices in there that I think will save you
headaches later.

The most important is the dock anchor system. Give every dock an Empty
called SD_BikeDockAnchor and every bike a corresponding SB_DockAnchor.
Codex can line those up exactly in Blender. Then when your game code
says “return bike”, you don’t need to work out wheel coordinates or
manually tweak rotation. You just snap the bicycle’s docking transform
onto the dock’s anchor.

I’d also make the bike more mechanically separated than most of the
buildings we’ve been doing. The front wheel, rear wheel,
handlebars/steering root and crank absolutely should not be joined into
the main frame. Those are the bits the game will need to animate.

The rear light is also worth taking seriously. Once you have night
cycling, a little blinking or constant red rear lamp, reflective
wheel/frame strips and a weak front beam are going to do a lot for
making the bikes feel like real objects inhabiting your nocturnal city
rather than merely a movement mechanic.

And the open basket is particularly useful for this game. Because you’re
already building delivery mechanics, I can see it becoming visually
functional: the Nice Things bouquet could visibly sit in the basket
while you’re riding to the recipient, film could be carried there, or
other delivery objects could occupy it. So I would absolutely prevent
Codex from making the basket a closed solid block.
