NORTH ROAD PRESTON BUS SHELTER — TEXTURE PASS
Codex / Blender Texture Brief
Zealot of Harperhey

(Supplied by Daniel on 2026-09-13 with a ten-panel reference board: metal
frame, glass panel, roof/canopy, red bench rail, poster/advert, pavement,
road surface, interior roof light, sticker/graffiti decal sheet, base/ground
contact. The board itself was attached in conversation only and is not stored
in the repository.)

1. OBJECTIVE
Material and texture pass on the approved shelter. Do not remodel unless a very
small change is necessary for textures to read. Move away from the clean,
synthetic "3D model" look toward convincing British street infrastructure that
has spent years outdoors in Northern England: rain, traffic pollution, dust,
leaning, fingerprints, stickers, scratches, condensation, road grime, sun,
cleaning, vandalism, wet/dry cycles. Used, not ruined. Textures lean toward
photorealism; do not make them retro, low-resolution or cartoonish.

2. MACRO / MESO / MICRO
Every major material needs large tonal/weather variation, meso scratches,
streaks, patches, fingerprints and joint grime, and micro grain/roughness. No
single procedural noise over everything; materials age differently.

3. GLASS (CRITICAL)
Not perfect CG glass. Faint road film and grey/brown grime; rain streaks;
dried water marks; fingerprints at hand height; palm smears; cleaning streaks;
fine and isolated larger scratches; grime concentrated on bottom edges and
where glass meets the frame; subtle transparency and roughness variation.
Centres of exposed panels relatively clean, edges and corners dirtier.
Occasional sticker remnants (adhesive residue, cleaner rectangles, torn paper)
without covering every surface. Imperfect night reflections that vary from
almost invisible to strongly reflective with viewing angle.

4. GLASS WATER
Wet-condition compatibility: streaks, droplets, thin wet patches, dried mineral
marks. Optional wetness mask/parameter to move between dry and rain. Never
permanently soaking.

5. METAL FRAME
Powder-coated municipal steel/aluminium, not plastic: orange peel, fine
scratches, colour inconsistency, edge wear, joint and bolt grime, rain streaks,
slight oxidation, fingerprints. Wear follows interaction: entrances, hand
height, lower frame, panel connections, exposed corners; less on protected
upper surfaces.

6. PAINTED METAL
Distinguish paint from metal beneath with colour, roughness and normal, small
justified exposed-metal regions. Restrained chipping — municipal, not derelict.

7. LOWER-LEVEL ROAD GRIME (VERY IMPORTANT)
Bottom 30-50 cm substantially dirtier: splashback, darkened grime, muddy
residue, aggregate particles, salt/dust, splash patterns, irregular build-up,
increasing toward ground via world-space/painted masks. Not a uniform gradient.

8. ROOF / CANOPY
Rain staining, water channels, atmospheric dirt, darker drainage paths, very
sparse bird dirt, algae/moss hints in damp seams, grime at edges and drainage
points. Upward surfaces dirtier than protected verticals. Underside cleaner
with insects/dust, subtle staining, grime around fixtures.

9. BOLTS AND FASTENERS
Different roughness from frame, scratches, recess dirt, occasional mild
oxidation, contact shadows, small variation between bolts.

10. ADVERTISING / INFORMATION PANELS
Paper/fabric texture, fading, creases, dirt, colour degradation, print grain,
imperfect edges. Behind glass: glass and print visually distinct layers, glass
reflections over the print.

11. STICKERS AND EPHEMERA
Small reusable decal library: torn sticker, shipping-label fragment, partially
removed sticker, adhesive rectangle, faded event sticker, small marker tag,
scratched initials, tape residue. Subtle; evidence of use, not a graffiti prop.

12. SEATING
Own material history: polished high-contact areas, scratches, grime in corners,
scuffs, dirt underneath; top wears differently from underside.

13. PAVEMENT CONTACT (ESSENTIAL)
Darker damp, dirt, grime around mounting plates, plausible moss/algae, rust
staining, trapped dust, splashback. It must not look placed on the pavement.

14. ROUGHNESS OVER COLOUR
Realism carried mostly by roughness: fingerprints, dried rain (+ subtle normal),
skin oil (smoother), road grime (rougher/darker), scratches (roughness +
normal), wet (much lower), clean vs dirty glass. Detail must survive
monochromatic night light.

15. NORMAL DETAIL
Physically plausible, subtle normals for powder coat, scratches, glass residue,
paint, paper, ground-contact dirt. No carved-looking scratches.

16. RESOLUTION
2K-4K for primary shelter materials where justified; 512-1K decal atlas; no
4K for bolts; efficient atlases.

17. UVS
Consistent texel density, no stretching, no mirrored dirt, logical seams,
unique space for major weathering, efficient packing, no repeated grime across
glass panels.

18. PBR MAPS
BaseColor, Normal, Roughness, Metallic, AO; ORM for GLB where appropriate;
alpha/transmission for glass; nothing that fails to translate to glTF/Three.js.

19. NIGHT APPEARANCE
Test cool LED, warm streetlight, shopfront spill, near darkness, wet pavement,
headlights, strong side light, backlight. Hard side light is most important:
scratches, prints, rain marks, dirty glass, powder coat and grime appear, then
disappear as the angle changes.

20. WET NIGHT VARIANT
Optional wetness system: selective metal roughness drop, stronger glass
streaking, darker lower surfaces and contact dirt, stronger reflections, water
mostly on horizontal surfaces, sheltered undersides drier. Respect gravity and
shelter; never lower roughness globally.

21. PERFORMANCE
Three.js. No dozens of transparent overlays, no dynamic lights, no unnecessary
geometry, no stacked expensive materials, no huge textures everywhere. Prefer
good UVs, high-quality PBR, decals, roughness variation, normals and masks.

22. DO NOT MAKE IT "GAME DIRTY"
No enormous scratches, blackened corners, uniform edge wear, random rust or
grunge everywhere. Weathering must say why it exists: rain moves down, water
collects horizontally, hands touch predictable places, spray comes up, dirt
gathers in recesses, sun affects exposed faces, cleaning leaves streaks.

23. PHOTOGRAPHIC TARGET
Something Daniel could have photographed for Public Illumination Studies.
Under direct streetlight briefly theatrical — bright glass, hard metal
highlights, deep shadow, visible grime — without stylising the object.

24. FINAL VALIDATION
Renders: 01 neutral daylight, 02 overcast daylight, 03 clean night, 04 hard LED
streetlight, 05 warm streetlight, 06 wet night, 07 close-up glass, 08 close-up
frame, 09 close-up lower grime, 10 grazing-light material test, 11 gameplay
camera distance, 12 extreme close-up.
Check: CG glass? plastic metal? motivated dirt? roughness visible under grazing
light? shelter meets pavement? detectable repetition? convincing from the
gameplay camera? Revise before export if any answer exposes the asset.
