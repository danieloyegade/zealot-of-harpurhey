NORTH ROAD PRESTON BUS SHELTER

Hero architectural/prop reconstruction for Zealot of Harpurhey

Create an accurate Blender reconstruction of the bus shelter and
shopping trolley shown in the supplied reference photograph.

This photograph is an important visual reference for the artistic
language of Zealot of Harpurhey and should be treated as a hero asset,
not as generic street furniture.

The goal is to reconstruct the physical objects as faithfully as
possible so that a later material, decal and lighting pass can reproduce
the original photograph closely.

IMPORTANT

This task is primarily a GEOMETRY PASS.

Do not create final photographic textures yet.

However, create properly separated surfaces and named objects for:

bus timetable;

No Smoking notice;

shelter identification number;

advertising panel;

glass;

lighting;

so that accurate decals and materials can be applied later.

SUCCESS CRITERION

The bus shelter should exist as a believable transparent physical
structure in three-dimensional space.

The shopping trolley should exist as an entirely independent object
positioned in front of it.

Nothing visible through the glass in the original photograph should be
mistaken for part of the bus shelter itself.

The finished untextured model should match the geometry and composition
of the source photograph closely enough that a later material and
lighting pass can recreate the photograph without changing the
underlying model.

There are three things I’d be especially strict about when you review
Codex’s first render.

First, ask for a render from behind the bus stop. That’s probably the
easiest diagnostic for the exact mistake it made before. If you rotate
the camera 180 degrees and suddenly discover trees, cars or a photograph
floating on the glass, the asset has been built incorrectly. From every
angle it should simply be a transparent glass shelter.

Second, I’d actually make three separate deliverable concepts, even
though two of them can travel together:

PrestonBusShelter.glb contains the structure, glass, bench/rail,
timetable holders, advert casing and lighting anchors.

PrestonTrolley.glb contains only the trolley.

Then the scene decides that the trolley happens to be sitting in front
of that bus stop. This matters because later you can reuse the exact
trolley elsewhere, remove it, move it six feet down the road, have an
NPC push it, or have it mysteriously disappear at another time of night.
It stops being baked into the photograph and becomes an object in the
world.

Third, the reflection should never be a texture pretending to be a
reflection. Once this reaches the material stage, I’d have the advert
genuinely emissive and give the shelter glass a physically plausible
transmissive/reflective material. Then, depending on how you implement
reflections in Three.js, that intense pale pink advert can genuinely
appear in the glass. That is precisely the distinction between
recreating the objects that produced the photograph and simply putting
the photograph into a game.
