BOUGAINVILLEA FENCE SCENE — MODULAR ASSET SET Codex / Blender Asset
Build Prompt Reference-led, reusable GLB assets for Zealot of Harperhay

1.  ROLE AND GOAL

You are building a modular set of production-ready Blender assets based
closely on the supplied reference photograph.

The reference image is the primary visual authority.

This is NOT one merged scene asset. Build the principal elements as
separate, independently placeable assets so they can later be assembled
between two buildings in the game.

Required separate assets:

A. BGV_signpost_no_entry B. BGV_fence_bougainvillea C.
BGV_background_tree D. BGV_ground_strip_optional

The assets should work together to reproduce the visual character of the
photograph, but each must have its own sensible origin, transforms,
materials and exportability.

The central visual idea is the contrast between ordinary municipal
infrastructure and unexpectedly lush flowering vegetation: a grey
traffic-sign pole, weathered timber fence, intense pink bougainvillea,
dense dark-green foliage, and hard directional sunlight creating
intricate botanical shadows.

The finished result should occupy the established Zealot of Harperhay
visual space between photographic reality and a game environment. It
should be materially convincing and recognisably based on the photograph
without becoming over-polished, plastic, decorative, or generically
“pretty.”

2.  REFERENCE INTERPRETATION

Study the supplied photograph carefully.

The composition contains: - a tall grey municipal pole; - a
square-backed traffic sign mounted high on the pole; - the sign face
contains a circular “No Entry” symbol; - a shallow circular lamp/light
fitting immediately above the sign; - a tapered/thicker lower pole
base; - a long vertical-board timber fence; - bougainvillea growing
irregularly across and above the fence; - clusters of saturated pink
flowers; - thin woody stems and climbing branches; - small-to-medium
green leaves; - dense trees and shrubs behind the fence; - very dark
foliage recesses; - strong sunlight from above/left; - complex leaf and
flower shadows projected onto the timber; - a narrow rough ground strip
beside the kerb.

Do not turn this into a manicured garden.

The vegetation should feel as though it has grown opportunistically
through an ordinary urban boundary.

3.  MODULAR ASSET STRUCTURE

Keep the following systems separate.

ASSET A — SIGNPOST / NO-ENTRY SIGN / LAMP One reusable object hierarchy
containing: - municipal pole; - enlarged/tapered lower base; - square
sign plate; - No Entry graphic; - upper circular lamp/light housing; -
mounting hardware.

ASSET B — FENCE + BOUGAINVILLEA One modular fence section with: - timber
boards; - horizontal structural rails where needed; - climbing woody
stems; - leaf clusters; - flower clusters; - foliage extending above and
beyond the fence silhouette.

The vegetation may be internally separated into sub-objects, but the
complete fence/flower combination should be exportable as one convenient
asset.

ASSET C — BACKGROUND TREE A separate dense tree/large shrub asset
designed to sit behind the fence and provide the deep green canopy
visible in the reference.

ASSET D — OPTIONAL GROUND STRIP A small modular strip containing the
rough soil/pavement transition immediately below the fence, if useful.

Do not merge the signpost into the fence. Do not merge the background
tree into the bougainvillea. Do not permanently attach the ground to
either building or fence.

4.  ASSET A — MUNICIPAL SIGNPOST

Reconstruct the pole closely from the photograph.

The pole is tall, narrow and utilitarian, painted/desaturated grey.

Important characteristics: - straight cylindrical main shaft; - slightly
thicker/tapered lower housing or base; - subtle seams/joints in the
lower section; - plausible metal thickness; - restrained roughness; -
slight dirt and age variation; - no glossy chrome appearance.

The pole should look like real painted or galvanised municipal street
furniture.

Use enough radial segments to produce a clean silhouette at normal
gameplay distance without excessive geometry.

5.  SIGN PLATE

The sign plate is a vertically oriented square/near-square panel with
softly rounded corners.

The photograph is strongly affected by sunlight, so do not interpret the
washed-out colours literally.

Construct: - rigid metal sign plate; - rounded corners; - shallow real
thickness; - rear mounting hardware/brackets; - front graphic as a clean
texture/decal.

The graphic should reproduce the recognisable No Entry road-sign
design: - circular field; - horizontal rectangular bar; - simple
high-contrast municipal graphic.

Do not bake the extreme photographic overexposure into the texture.

Instead, create a physically plausible sign material that can become
bright under direct light.

The sign should have: - mildly weathered reflective/painted surface; -
subtle dirt; - faint surface ageing; - no exaggerated rust; - no
perfectly pristine CGI finish.

6.  UPPER LAMP / LIGHT FITTING

Immediately above the sign is a shallow circular lamp/light housing.

Model this as actual geometry.

Pay attention to: - broad flattened circular/domed form; - short
mounting connection to the pole; - underside/lens area; - slightly aged
off-white/grey housing.

It should cast and receive believable shadows.

Do not model it as a floating disc.

7.  ASSET B — TIMBER FENCE

The fence is a critical material and lighting surface.

Build a modular section of vertically arranged timber boards.

The boards should not be one perfectly flat wall.

Include: - individual plank divisions; - very subtle differences in
depth and alignment; - narrow gaps between selected boards; - irregular
top edges; - visible vertical wood grain; - occasional knots; - splits
and surface checking used sparingly; - screw/nail details only where
visible/useful; - plausible board thickness.

The timber is aged grey-brown rather than fresh orange lumber.

Material qualities: - predominantly matte; - sun-bleached vertical
faces; - darker recesses/gaps; - subtle green/organic staining near
ground level; - uneven weather exposure; - fine rough wood normal
detail.

Avoid: - glossy wood; - cartoon knots; - uniform procedural planks; -
identical repeated boards; - exaggerated displacement.

The fence needs enough geometric relief that directional sunlight
produces narrow shadows along plank edges.

8.  BOUGAINVILLEA — PRIMARY VEGETATION REQUIREMENT

The flowering plant is the visual focus of this asset set.

Do NOT make it as a generic spherical flowering bush.

It must read as a climbing, sprawling bougainvillea-like plant growing
across a fence.

Its structure should contain three distinct scales:

A. PRIMARY WOODY STRUCTURE - thin, dark woody stems; - irregular
branching; - some long horizontal/diagonal stems; - stems that visibly
trace across the timber fence; - occasional bare stretches; - branches
that project away from the fence.

B. LEAF STRUCTURE - relatively small green leaves; - leaves distributed
in clusters along branches; - variable orientation; - non-uniform
density; - some sparse areas where stems remain visible; - foliage
projecting both in front of and above the fence.

C. FLOWER / BRACT CLUSTERS - saturated pink to magenta clusters; -
irregular clustered masses rather than individual evenly spaced
flowers; - different cluster sizes; - some large dense flowering
zones; - smaller isolated clusters further along the stems; - natural
asymmetry.

The photograph shows a major concentration of flowers around the
upper-middle/left region, with branching growth spreading horizontally
toward the right and smaller blooms appearing farther down and outward.

Preserve that asymmetric rhythm.

9.  FLOWER CONSTRUCTION

The flowers must remain visually convincing without becoming
prohibitively expensive for Three.js.

Use a hybrid method: - a small library of low-poly bract/flower cluster
meshes; - alpha-cutout cards where appropriate; - instancing for
repeated cluster families; - controlled random rotation, scale and hue
variation.

Do not use one flat billboard for the entire flowering plant.

At close-to-medium distance, clusters need enough layered depth to cast
fragmented shadows and retain volume when viewed obliquely.

Create approximately 5–10 distinct flower-cluster variants before
instancing/variation so repetition is not obvious.

The colour should be rich pink/magenta with variation between: - sunlit
pale pink; - saturated mid pink; - deeper shadowed crimson/magenta.

Do not make every flower identical in colour.

10. LEAF CONSTRUCTION

Leaves should similarly use efficient repeated geometry.

Use: - several leaf/leaf-sprig variants; - alpha cutouts or low-poly
leaves; - instancing; - varied orientation; - restrained scale
variation.

Leaf materials should have believable front/back behaviour where
practical.

Avoid perfectly opaque plastic leaves.

Use subtle translucency/transmission or an efficient approximation if
compatible with the existing Three.js pipeline, but prioritise
performance and stable rendering.

Leaves should have enough geometric separation to generate broken,
recognisable botanical shadows.

11. VEGETATION DEPTH

The bougainvillea must not appear painted onto the fence.

Create multiple depth layers: - stems close to the timber; - foliage
2–10 cm forward; - major branches and flower clusters extending
farther; - occasional stems extending above the fence; - a few branches
reaching laterally beyond the main mass.

The shadows and parallax created by this depth are essential.

The fence should remain visible through the plant in many areas.

Do not cover the entire fence uniformly.

12. ASSET C — BACKGROUND TREE / DENSE FOLIAGE

Create a separate background tree or large shrub mass.

Its purpose is both architectural and tonal.

In the reference, the foliage behind the fence creates a dense, dark
green wall with pockets approaching near-black shadow. Sunlit leaves
appear around the upper-left and upper-middle areas while the
right/central recess becomes much darker.

The tree should contain: - believable trunk/major branch support where
visible; - broad irregular canopy; - layered leaf clusters; - variable
leaf size/orientation; - non-spherical silhouette; - internal depth.

Do not make a generic perfect ball-shaped game tree.

The canopy should be suitable for positioning behind and above the
fence, partly filling the gap between two future buildings.

Create a dense enough silhouette that it can form a dark visual backdrop
behind the pink flowers.

13. SHADOWS — CRITICAL REQUIREMENT

The shadow behaviour in the reference is one of the most important
aspects of the scene.

DO NOT bake the photograph’s shadows directly into the fence texture.

The asset geometry must be capable of producing comparable shadows
dynamically.

The reference contains: - hard-to-medium edged directional sunlight; -
leaf shadows projected across multiple timber boards; - thin branch
shadows; - dense overlapping flower/leaf silhouettes; - bright patches
alternating with deep shade; - large areas of the foliage falling into
near-black shadow.

The bougainvillea should therefore have enough separated geometry to
create intricate projected shadows.

Validate the scene using a strong directional/sun light from above and
camera-left/front-left, adjusted to approximate the reference.

The shadow pattern should feel fragmented, botanical and spatially
layered.

Do not fake the entire shadow with a single opacity decal.

However, for performance, a supplementary low-cost shadow/decal solution
may be created as an OPTIONAL distant LOD, provided the close version
uses real geometry-driven shadows.

14. LIGHTING VALIDATION

Create a reference-comparison lighting setup in Blender for validation
only.

Target: - strong summer daylight; - high contrast; - bright upper-left
illumination; - deep foliage shadows; - clear projected botanical
shadows on the fence; - strong but plausible specular/reflective
response from the road sign; - no flat studio lighting.

The photograph has a slightly heightened, almost uncanny contrast.
Preserve this feeling without baking a stylised grade into the material
textures.

The asset must still work under the game’s normal night lighting.

Also create a second night validation render showing how the fence,
flowers, tree and sign behave under a nearby streetlight.

15. THE SPECTRES ARE ALL AROUND US / GAME AESTHETIC

This scene is particularly important to the visual language of Zealot of
Harperhay.

The combination of: - municipal sign; - ordinary timber boundary; -
aggressive artificial infrastructure; - luxuriant flowers; - deep
shadow; - isolated pool of illumination; - banal urban space becoming
unexpectedly beautiful

fits the broader The Spectres Are All Around Us practice.

Do not exaggerate this with fantasy objects.

The uncanniness should emerge from accurate observation, framing,
contrast and the strange coexistence of municipal infrastructure and
organic growth.

The final asset should be suitable for: - normal gameplay; - planimetric
compositions; - scripted cinematic scenes; - screen-recorded film
sequences; - daylight and nocturnal versions of the world.

16. PLACEMENT BETWEEN BUILDINGS

This fence scene will eventually occupy a gap between two buildings.

Design accordingly.

The fence should: - have a clean modular width; - be
extendable/repeatable; - terminate cleanly at both ends; - tolerate
partial occlusion by adjacent architecture; - have no permanently
modelled buildings attached; - allow the tree canopy to rise behind the
gap; - allow the bougainvillea silhouette to overlap the neighbouring
building edges naturally.

Provide at least: - one hero fence section based closely on the
photograph; - one simpler extension section if useful; - optional
left/right end modules if needed.

Avoid obvious repetition when multiple fence sections are placed
together.

17. GROUND / KERB INTERACTION

If building the optional ground strip, reproduce the rough transition
visible in the reference: - narrow pavement edge; - kerb; - dark
soil/moss near the fence; - small weeds; - rough granular
asphalt/concrete; - minor organic debris.

Keep weeds and debris as separate or efficiently instanced details where
possible.

The ground should not become the main asset.

Its purpose is to make the fence feel physically rooted rather than
floating.

20. ORIGINS AND MODULARITY

Set sensible origins.

Signpost: origin at ground centre of pole.

Fence/flowers: origin at ground level on the rear/centre line of the
fence module.

Tree: origin at trunk/base ground contact.

Ground: origin at one modular corner or centre according to the
project’s established environment convention.

Apply transforms before export.

All major assets must be independently movable in Three.js.

21. THREE.JS / GLB PERFORMANCE

Build for practical real-time use.

Requirements: - glTF 2.0 compatible materials; - efficient topology; -
no unnecessary hidden faces; - clean normals; - applied transforms; -
sensible texture resolutions; - texture atlases where useful; -
instancing/repeated mesh strategy for leaves and flowers; - minimal
transparent overdraw; - avoid extremely dense geometry for every leaf; -
LODs for vegetation if useful.

The hero version may be relatively detailed because this is a visually
distinctive scene, but it must remain appropriate for a browser-based
Three.js environment.

Suggested LOD philosophy:

LOD0: Full branch structure, layered leaves, layered flower clusters,
real shadow casting.

LOD1: Reduced leaf/flower geometry and simplified branches.

LOD2: Cluster cards/impostor-style vegetation while preserving overall
silhouette and colour distribution.

The timber fence should remain geometric at all practical LODs.

22. DO NOT DO

Do not: - merge all assets into one object; - make the bougainvillea a
generic flower bush; - make the tree a spherical game tree; - make the
fence perfectly new; - use flat brown rectangles for the timber; - bake
the hero shadows into the albedo; - make every flower identical; - make
every leaf identical; - use excessive gloss; - create plastic-looking
vegetation; - over-saturate the timber; - add fantasy embellishments; -
over-clean the ground; - create an architectural-visualisation
aesthetic; - simplify the signpost into an untextured cylinder and
rectangle; - omit the small lamp above the sign.

26. FINAL PRIORITY ORDER

When compromises are necessary, prioritise:

1.  Bougainvillea silhouette, branching structure and pink flower
    distribution.
2.  Real geometry-driven leaf/flower shadows across the fence.
3.  Weathered timber fence texture and depth.
4.  Accurate municipal signpost/sign/lamp silhouette.
5.  Dense dark-green background tree and canopy depth.
6.  Correct spatial separation between plant, fence and background
    foliage.
7.  Efficient Three.js performance.
8.  Minor ground details.

The finished scene should feel like an observed fragment of an ordinary
North Manchester street that has become strangely theatrical through
flowers, municipal infrastructure, sunlight and shadow.

It should not look like a generic garden asset pack.

It should look specific enough that, when assembled between two
buildings in Zealot of Harperhay, it retains the photographic
strangeness and beauty of the supplied reference image.
