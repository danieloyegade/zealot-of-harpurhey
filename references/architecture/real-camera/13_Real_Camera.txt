REAL CAMERA, SEVENDALE HOUSE, MANCHESTER

Detailed untextured architectural reconstruction for Zealot of Harperhey

Create an accurate, game-ready Blender reconstruction of Real Camera at
Sevendale House, Manchester, based on ALL supplied reference
photographs.

This asset is for Zealot of Harperhey, a Three.js game.

The defining feature of this building is its ornate red sandstone /
terracotta architecture.

Do NOT simplify this into a flat red-brick shopfront.

The modelling priority is:

1.  architectural form;

2.  decorative facade depth;

3.  shopfront proportions;

4.  corner massing;

5.  windows;

6.  Real Camera storefront;

7.  signage-ready surfaces;

8.  later texture/detail work.

This pass is primarily GEOMETRY ONLY.

Do not create final textures, photographic signage, grime, graffiti,
glass reflections or detailed shop stock yet.

⸻

1.  REFERENCE STRATEGY

Use all supplied references together.

They show:

-   frontal Real Camera facade;

-   oblique lower facade;

-   corner bay;

-   arched/rounded window openings;

2.  SCOPE

Build enough of Sevendale House around Real Camera that the shop feels
embedded in a real building rather than floating inside an isolated
shopfront.

The asset should include:

-   Real Camera shop frontage;

-   adjacent Real Camera Shopping Centre & Gallery entrance;

-   lower two to three storeys of the surrounding facade;

-   corner bay / corner return;

-   enough upper facade to establish the building’s identity;

-   projecting clock;

-   major architectural ornament.

Full detailed interiors are not required yet.

⸻

Highest geometry fidelity:

4.  DO NOT FLATTEN THE ORNAMENT

Many facade elements must exist as real geometry.

Model as geometry:

-   major cornices;

-   lintels;

-   sills;

-   pilasters;

-   column-like vertical projections;

-   horizontal moulding bands;

-   recessed arches;

-   capitals;

-   carved blocks;

-   bay-window projections;

-   corner chamfers/curves;

-   deep window reveals;

-   ground-floor rustication;

Do not solve all of this with a single normal map.

⸻

5.  ORNAMENT RULE

Use this test:

If the detail creates a clearly visible shadow or changes the silhouette
from pavement distance, model it.

If it is fine engraving, weathering, cracks or shallow carving, leave it
for later texture/normal work.

18. CORNER ARCHITECTURE

The building corner is highly articulated.

Preserve:

-   layered mouldings;

-   horizontal string courses.

19. PILASTERS

The facade repeatedly uses strong vertical pilaster-like projections.

20. BASES

The ground-floor stone bases are especially chunky and visually

Include:

-   battered/projecting bases;

-   horizontal ledges.

⸻

21. CAPITALS / ORNAMENTAL HEADS

Several facade piers terminate in carved or stepped heads.

Build simplified but recognisable forms.

Do not attempt to reproduce every tiny carving.

Use:

-   large block shape;

-   stepped profile;

-   visible fluting/groove where strong.

Fine carving can come later through normals.

⸻

22. HORIZONTAL STRING COURSES

-   recesses;

-   stepped moulding profile.

40. THREE.JS PERFORMANCE

time.

Spend polygons on:

-   silhouette;

-   corners;

-   mouldings;

-   deep recesses;

-   shopfront;

-   distinctive ornament.

-   flat wall surfaces;

-   hidden backs;

-   tiny carvings;

-   masonry joints;

-   shutter grooves;

-   microscopic window details.

44. ORNAMENT PASS

After blockout approval, add the architectural details.

Focus on:

-   pilaster bases;

-   capitals;

-   stepped mouldings;

-   horizontal string courses;

-   window surrounds;

-   arched heads;

-   bay-window ledges;

-   ground-floor rustication;

-   cornice profiles;

-   facade recesses.

This pass is where the building should begin to feel unmistakably like

⸻

45. ORNAMENT MUST HAVE DEPTH

Do not make mouldings as flat lines drawn on the wall.

Give them enough projection to create:

-   highlight;

-   shadow;

-   depth;

-   parallax when the player walks past.

This matters particularly because the game camera can approach close to
the building.

⸻

46. TEXTURE PASS IS LATER

Stop before:

-   realistic stone texture;

-   red/brown colour variation;

-   dirt;

-   graffiti;

-   typography;

-   window reflections;

-   shop stock.

The current deliverable is a strong architectural model.

⸻

47. OPTIONAL DECORATION DETAIL LEVEL

If some ornament is too complex for efficient game geometry, simplify
its shape, but preserve:

-   location;

-   projection;

-   scale;

-   visual rhythm.

A simplified carved capital with correct silhouette is preferable to a
completely flat wall.

⸻

48. COLLISION

Collision will be handled primarily in-game.

Ensure the facade architecture is physically coherent, particularly:

-   stairs;

-   recessed entrance;

-   protruding bases;

-   projecting signs.

Do not create unnecessarily complex collision meshes inside Blender.

50. FINAL CHECK

The untextured clay model should still communicate:

-   ornate historic Manchester architecture;

-   deep stone articulation;

-   prominent vertical pilasters;

-   projecting bay windows;

-   strong horizontal moulding bands;

-   distinctive Real Camera storefront;

-   recessed gallery entrance;

-   projecting shop sign;

-   corner clock.

If the model only becomes recognisable after applying a photograph as a
texture, the geometry is not detailed enough.

⸻

SUCCESS CRITERION

Real Camera should feel carved out of a substantial red sandstone
historic building, not pasted onto a rectangular facade.

A player walking along the pavement should perceive real parallax and
shadow across:

-   columns/pilasters;

-   window surrounds;

-   mouldings;

-   cornices;

-   bases;

-   arches;

-   bay windows.

Preserve the building’s architectural flourishes even where they require
more geometry than the simpler modern buildings elsewhere in the game.
